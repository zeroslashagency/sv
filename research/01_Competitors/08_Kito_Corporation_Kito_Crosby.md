## 1. Executive Summary

Kito Corporation is a Japanese global leader in hoists and cranes, founded 1932. In **January 2023** it combined with The Crosby Group (a US/UK lifting-and-rigging leader dating to 1867) to form **Kito Crosby**, a ~$1.1 billion-revenue multinational headquartered in Arlington, Texas. Kito Crosby was owned by KKR. As of **February 2026, Kito Crosby was acquired by Columbus McKinnon Corporation (Nasdaq: CMCO)** for ~$2.7 billion — creating one of the largest material-handling groups in the world (subject to a DOJ-required divestiture of overlapping chain-hoist assets).

Kito is a **product-brand and precision-engineering powerhouse** — chain hoists, wire rope hoists, cranes, chain, and rigging hardware sold through a global distributor network — rather than a bespoke heavy-EOT-crane fabricator like SVMH. However, **Kito India (Bangalore)** puts the brand directly in SVMH's home city, and Kito's hoists/crane components are among the premium products SVMH-class crane builders both compete against and integrate.

**Confidence: High** for structure, ownership, products, locations; **Medium** for some certification specifics; noted inline.

## 3. Brands Portfolio

Kito Crosby operates as an umbrella over leading product brands:

- **Kito** — electric/manual/lever chain hoists, wire rope hoists, cranes (since 1932)
- **Crosby** — lifting, rigging & securement hardware (shackles, clamps, hooks, blocks; since 1867)
- **Harrington** — hoists, cranes, below-the-hook (US market brand since 1867; Kito's US manufacturing/sales arm, Manheim PA)
- **Gunnebo Industries** — chain, slings, offshore lifting (since 1764)
- **Peerless** — rigging, cargo securement, marine, traction chain
- **eepos** — flexible aluminum crane systems / workstation cranes (German; acquired Aug 2024 for ~€37.7M / $41.7M)

## 4. Product Categories

**Kito-brand core line (kito.co.jp):**
- Electric chain hoists (EH series)
- Wire rope hoists (RH / RY series — incl. 10t double-girder RY hoist launched in Asia 2025)
- Lever hoists (LB series)
- Hand chain hoists (CB series)
- Air hoists (pneumatic)
- Cranes and components (overhead/workstation crane kits, end trucks, components)
- Slings / below-the-hook devices; large-capacity round slings (RG, 3.2–8t, launched 2025)

**Kito Crosby group-wide:** power chain hoists, wire rope hoists, manual hoists, overhead & workstation cranes, aluminum crane systems (eepos), forged rigging hardware (shackles, hooks, links), lifting/alloy chain, wire rope & synthetic slings, cargo/load securement, offshore & subsea lifting, spark-resistant (Ex-certified) air hoists.

Note: Kito focuses on **catalog/standardized precision products** (hoists + rigging) plus flexible/custom crane systems — distinct from SVMH's engineered heavy EOT/gantry/ladle-crane fabrication. Founder motto: "Value both the standard and custom-made products."

## 6. Manufacturing Capabilities

- **In-house vertical production** — design, development, part production, assembly, and final product testing all conducted in-house (Kito corporate philosophy).
- Precision-engineered forged components; alloy-steel overhead lifting chain (forged alloy steel meeting ASTM standards — per DOJ complaint).
- Kito and Columbus McKinnon named by DOJ as the **two leading US manufacturers of electric chain hoists and overhead lifting chain** (hence the required divestiture).
- Manufacturing footprint spans Japan, China, Thailand, USA, Canada, Brazil, Germany, Italy, Finland, Netherlands.

## 10. Years in Business / History Highlights

- **1932** — Kito founded, Omori, Tokyo; developed first electric chain hoist in postwar Japan.
- **1990** — US subsidiary founded (start of overseas expansion).
- **1998 / 2001** — ISO 9001 / ISO 14001 certifications.
- **2007** — Acquired by The Carlyle Group.
- **2023 (Jan)** — Crosby Group + Kito Corporation combine to form **Kito Crosby** (under KKR ownership).
- **2024 (Aug)** — Acquired eepos GmbH (Germany).
- **2025** — Product launches: 10t double-girder RY hoist (Asia), large-capacity RG round slings.
- **2025 (Feb)** — Columbus McKinnon agreed to acquire Kito Crosby (~$2.7B).
- **2026 (Jan)** — DOJ requires CMCO divestiture (power chain hoist + chain businesses to Pacific Avenue Capital Partners).
- **2026 (Feb)** — CMCO completes acquisition of Kito Crosby.

## 15. Threat to SVMH

**Overall threat: Moderate — indirect but locally present.**

- **Different core model.** Kito is a premium **standardized hoist + rigging** manufacturer selling through distributors, not a bespoke heavy-EOT/gantry/ladle-crane fabricator. It does not compete head-on for SVMH's engineered-crane fabrication and AMC contracts.
- **Component-level overlap is real.** SVMH sells crane components/spares — **crab units, hoists, DSL busbars, gearboxes**. Kito India explicitly sells **chain hoists, crane components, and busbar systems in Bangalore**, the same city and product adjacency. Kito is a premium branded alternative for the exact hoist/component parts SVMH supplies or integrates.
- **Brand and scale gulf.** Kito Crosby (now Columbus McKinnon) is a ~$1.1B global player with polished digital assets, 3D/CAD tools, deep catalogs, and 3,500+ distributors — dwarfing SVMH's ~₹13.6 Cr revenue and weak IndiaMART-dependent web presence. On any digital/credibility comparison, Kito is a benchmark to aspire to, not a like-for-like rival.
- **Positioning takeaway for SVMH's website:** Emphasize what Kito cannot easily offer in India — **fully engineered custom cranes (IS 807/IS 3177/FEM 9.511) with in-house fabrication, local AMC/service, and fast spares** — while noting SVMH can integrate premium hoists (incl. Kito-class) into its cranes. Kito's clean product-catalog UX, CAD/3D downloads, and industry-segmented navigation are strong models to emulate for a superior SVMH site.
