## 1. Executive Summary

Columbus McKinnon (CMCO) is a US-headquartered, publicly traded (Nasdaq: CMCO) global leader in "intelligent motion solutions for material handling" — hoists, crane systems, crane components, rigging/lifting hardware, precision conveyance, and digital power/motion control. It is one of the largest players in the global lifting equipment industry, roughly **two orders of magnitude larger than SVMH** by revenue.

The defining recent event: on **February 3-4, 2026, CMCO closed its ~transformational acquisition of Kito Crosby** (from KKR), combining two of the biggest names in lifting. This roughly doubles the business toward a ~$2B+ pro-forma revenue scale and adds the iconic Kito, Crosby, Harrington, Gunnebo, and Peerless brands. Post-acquisition FY2026 net sales were **$1.2 billion (up 24%)**.

CMCO is not a direct like-for-like competitor to SVMH's custom EOT/gantry fabrication business, but it is a formidable **component and brand competitor** (hoists, wire rope hoists, crane kits, DSL-equivalent controls, chain, hooks) — and its Yale / CM / STAHL / Kito brands are sold in India, including via distributors, directly overlapping SVMH's crane-components and spares line.

## 15. Threat to SVMH

**Direct threat: Moderate, concentrated in components & premium brand pull.**

- **Component/spares overlap:** CMCO's Yale, CM, STAHL, and now Kito/Crosby/Harrington brands compete head-on with SVMH's spares line (hoists, wire rope hoists, forged hooks, chain, controls/DSL-equivalent radio+drive systems). Crosby is the global quality benchmark for forged hooks/shackles/rigging — a segment SVMH sells.
- **India presence:** Yale, STAHL, and Kito hoists are actively sold in India through distributors and IndiaMART-type channels — competing in SVMH's own home turf for standardized product.
- **Not a direct threat on custom fabrication:** SVMH's core (custom double-girder EOT, gantry, ladle/hot-metal cranes built to IS 807 / IS 3177 for local industrial clients, plus local AMC/service) is a fabrication + service business where a US/German-brand giant is uncompetitive on price and local responsiveness. CMCO does not do low-cost local fab or nearby AMC in Bengaluru.
- **Digital gap (the real lesson):** CMCO's website is a best-in-class benchmark — segmented by industry AND product, rich resource library, downloadable line cards/certificates, video library, clear brand architecture. SVMH's IndiaMART reliance is a competitive weakness this website project should directly close. Recommend SVMH borrow CMCO's structure: industry landing pages, product category grid with spec sheets, downloadable catalog/certificates, and case-study/blog content.

**Net:** Treat CMCO as an aspirational digital benchmark and a components-tier competitor for premium/imported spares, not as a direct rival for custom Indian crane fabrication and service contracts.
