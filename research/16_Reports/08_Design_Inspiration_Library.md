## Executive Summary
The visual language of premium crane/industrial brands converges on a repeatable formula: **"product-in-use over product-in-void," cinematic factory photography, numeric proof, and restrained line-icon systems.** SVMH can adopt this premium visual grammar at low cost, paired with a warm-industrial palette (already in the `sv.pen` design direction: Anton headlines, Inter/Geist body, warm concrete tones). This library organizes the best references into applicable categories for the Phase 2 design work.

## Recurring visual rules (apply site-wide)
1. **Product-in-use, not product-in-void** — every crane shown lifting real loads in a real bay.
2. **Numeric proof on imagery** — captions with capacity/span/industry (ABUS-style descriptive alt text doubles as SEO).
3. **Cinematic factory shooting** — wide, well-lit shots of welding, assembly, load-test; conveys capability text cannot.
4. **One dominant hero message + CTA** — resist carousels (LCP + clarity cost, per audits).
5. **Unified line-icon system** — avoid mixed icon styles (ElectroMech's mixed grid is the cautionary case).
6. **Restraint** — warm-industrial palette, generous whitespace, consistent radii; premium = calm, not busy.

## How this feeds Phase 2
This library maps directly onto the `sv.pen` design boards: hero slabs, product photography panels, factory/installation galleries, technical-readout bars, and the icon/typography system. Use the references as the mood-and-standard for art direction; use the "recurring rules" as the design QA checklist.
