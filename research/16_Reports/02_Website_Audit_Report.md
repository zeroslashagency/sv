# Website Audit Report — Cross-Competitor Synthesis (SVMH Phase 1)

**Prepared for:** SVMH, Bengaluru · **Date:** 2026-07-05
**Sources:** 8 full website audits in `02_Website_Audits/` (Konecranes, Demag, ABUS, Street Crane, GH Cranes, STAHL, Columbus McKinnon, Kito Crosby), each covering 18 dimensions.

## Executive Summary
Across the 8 audited leaders, a consistent pattern emerges: **world-class technical depth undermined by conversion friction and (for India) localization gaps.** Every site nails product engineering detail; almost none make it easy to get a quote from the product page, and most dilute their hero with carousels or brand-walls. The premium brands' biggest structural weakness — thin/absent India localization and price opacity — is precisely the opening SVMH should exploit. The best single pattern to copy is **dual-axis navigation (products × industries)**; the best pattern to avoid is **the multi-message carousel hero with no dominant CTA.**

## 2. Common strengths (best-in-class patterns to copy)
1. **Dual-axis navigation** (products AND industries) — GH, Demag, Kito. *Audits call this SVMH's "single highest-value copy."*
2. **Textbook technical product pages** — benefit → specs → variants → engineering detail → downloads → cross-sell (Demag DVR, Kito, Street Crane). Every claim carries a number.
3. **Proof density** — installs counters, named client logos, dated news, industry case studies (GH, CM, Demag).
4. **Configurators / 3D tooling** — Konecranes "Build & Quote", Demag Designer — self-service specification.
5. **Industry landing pages** with real application photography + linked case studies (CM, GH).

## 3. Common weaknesses (mistakes to avoid — and exploit)
1. **RFQ friction** — Konecranes/Demag/CM force a trip to a separate Contact page; Kito gates CAD behind a 7-field form + CAPTCHA. *Nobody offers a frictionless on-page, product-preselected RFQ.*
2. **Carousel/brand-wall heroes** with no dominant CTA — Kito, GH, ABUS, CM (hurts LCP + message clarity).
3. **Price & spec opacity** — specs trapped in images (Street, ABUS); no datasheets; no price guidance anywhere.
4. **Schema gaps** — Kito's 636 product pages have no Product/Offer schema; missed rich-results across the board.
5. **IA/URL hygiene debt** — Demag `/node/` IDs, ABUS URLs with spaces, GH empty slugs (`1425---/`), STAHL duplicate migration URLs.
6. **Weak India localization** — Konecranes /en-in is a thin shell; premium brands are Euro/US-first with no IS-code content, no ₹ guidance, no local service depth.

## 4. What SVMH should COPY vs AVOID
**COPY:** dual-axis nav · numeric technical product pages · on-page spec tables + downloadable datasheets · industry pages with case studies · trust/proof density · configurator-style tooling (lightweight).
**AVOID:** carousel heroes · burying the RFQ on a separate page · gating essential specs/CAD behind heavy forms · shipping a thin "shell" region site · messy auto-generated URLs · diffuse multi-CTA homepages.

## 5. The India opening (net conclusion)
The premium leaders are beatable *in India* on exactly the axes they neglect: **India-first localization (IS 807/3177/4137, ₹ pricing guidance, Bengaluru/Karnataka service), frictionless on-page RFQ + WhatsApp, transparent specs/datasheets, and clean fast pages.** No competitor — global or regional — currently does all of these together.
