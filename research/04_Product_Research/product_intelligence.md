# Product Intelligence — Industrial Crane Reference (SVMH Phase 1)

Per-crane-type reference for SVMH's portfolio: applications, industries, specs, capacities, standards, components, accessories, maintenance, safety, buying considerations, pain points, and buyer FAQs. Built from live research on 2026-07-05. Evidence tagged with Source URL / Confidence / Notes.

> **Standards anchor (India-centric).** SVMH's cranes are governed primarily by Indian Standards, cross-referenced to international duty codes:
> - **IS 3177:1999** — Code of practice for EOT & gantry cranes (other than steelwork); Annexure-A defines the buyer information set. *(Source: revacranes.com Buyer's Guide PDF, High)*
> - **IS 807:2006** — Design, erection & testing (structural portion) of cranes/hoists. Girder deflection limit: max vertical deflection ≤ 1/750 of span (span >12 m) or 1/600 (span <12 m), clause 20. *(Source: revacranes.com Buyer's Guide, High)*
> - **IS 13834 (Part 1 & 5):1994** — Crane classification (general / overhead & portal bridge). *(Source: revacranes.com, High)*
> - **IS 4137** — Code for heavy-duty EOT cranes (steelwork/ladle). *(Confidence: Medium — widely cited; confirm current revision)*
> - **FEM 9.511 / ISO M-classes** — duty groups M3–M8. **CMAA 70/74** — crane classes A–F. **HMI/ASME** — hoist classes H1–H5. *(Source: demagcranes.com duty-classification overview, High)*

## 1. EOT Single-Girder Crane

**Applications:** general lifting in workshops, light/medium fabrication, warehousing, assembly, maintenance bays. **Industries:** automotive ancillaries, general engineering, light manufacturing, packaging, MSME workshops.

**Specs:** capacity typically 0.5–20 T (economical sweet-spot ≤10–15 T); span to ~30 m; hoist runs on the bottom flange (underslung) or top of a single bridge beam; good headroom economy, lower building cost. **Duty:** M3–M6. *(Source: technomaxme.com, mazzellacompanies.com, High)*

**Components:** single bridge girder, end carriages, electric wire-rope or chain hoist + trolley (crab), LT/CT drives (S4 crane-duty motors), festoon/DSL power, pendant or radio control. **Accessories:** anti-collision, rail sweeps, limit switches, hooters/lights.

**Maintenance:** periodic hoist brake check, wire-rope inspection & lubrication, wheel/rail alignment, festoon/DSL condition, brake-torque verification (hoist brake 150% torque for single-girder single-brake, IS 3177 cl. 8.12). **Safety:** overload limiter, upper/lower hoist limits, earthing per IS 3043, 5:1 load-suspension factor (IS 3177 cl. 7.4.2). *(Source: revacranes.com, High)*

**Buying considerations:** lowest total cost for ≤15 T; limited hook approach/headroom vs double girder; not ideal for continuous severe duty. **Pain points:** buyers under-spec duty class → premature wear; ignore future capacity headroom.

**Buyer FAQ:** *Single or double girder?* → Single for ≤15 T & light-moderate duty; double above. *Max span?* ~30 m practical. *Cheaper?* Yes, 20–40% below double at same capacity <15 T.

## 2. EOT Double-Girder Crane

**Applications:** heavy-duty lifting, high spans, cranes needing walkways/cabins, auxiliary hoists, high hook lift. **Industries:** heavy fabrication, power plants, steel, cement, paper, defence, EPC.

**Specs:** capacity 5–100+ T (custom to 500 T); wide spans; trolley rides on top of two girders → higher hook lift & better hook approach; supports cabin, platform (single/both side, IS 3177 cl.), under-bridge/on-platform lighting. **Duty:** M5–M8. *(Source: mazzellacompanies.com "capacities over 15 T & spans over 65 ft," revacranes.com, High)*

**Components:** twin box/plate girders, heavy end carriages, main + auxiliary hoists, cross-travel crab, hardened helical gearboxes, S4 motors, thruster/electro-hydraulic brakes, DSL busbar power. **Accessories:** operator cabin, maintenance platform, walkway, anti-collision, load display, weighing.

**Maintenance:** girder deflection check (≤1/750 span), dual hoist brakes (125% torque each, IS 3177 cl. 8.12), gearbox oil, bearing inspection (cl. 8.7), wheel-load compliance (cl. 8.6.8). **Safety:** redundant brakes, overload protection, wire-rope factor per duty, ASME Sec IX welding qualification, IS 807 stress limits. *(Source: revacranes.com, High)*

**Buying considerations:** higher capital cost, more headroom needed; justified by capacity, duty, and hook geometry. **Pain points:** long lead time; foundation/gantry-beam readiness; underestimating power-supply/DSL needs.

**Buyer FAQ:** *When double girder?* >15 T or >20 m span or cabin/aux-hoist needed. *Aux hoist?* Common for precise placement of lighter loads alongside a heavy main hook.

## 3. Gantry & Portal Cranes

**Applications:** outdoor/indoor yards where no overhead runway exists — the crane carries its own legs on rails or wheels. Container/coil/precast handling, storage yards, fabrication yards. **Industries:** ports, precast concrete, steel yards, shipbuilding, infrastructure. *(Source: search corpus, High)*

**Specs:** full gantry (both legs on ground rails) or semi-gantry (one leg + one on elevated runway); single or double girder; capacity 1–100+ T; cantilever options; rail-mounted (RMG) or rubber-tyred (RTG) for container work. **Duty:** M4–M7.

**Components:** bridge, rigid + flexible legs, sill beams, travel drives, hoist/trolley, DSL or cable-reel power. **Accessories:** anti-collision, storm/rail clamps (outdoor), wind-load design, cabin.

**Maintenance:** rail & wheel alignment, storm-anchor checks, structural inspection of legs, drive maintenance. **Safety:** wind-load & storm brakes (outdoor), rail clamps, anti-derail, earthing. **Buying considerations:** outdoor cranes need wind/seismic design; RTG vs RMG mobility trade-off. **Pain points:** ground/rail preparation cost; outdoor corrosion (coastal).

**Buyer FAQ:** *Gantry vs overhead?* Gantry when there is no building structure to mount a runway. *Portal = gantry?* Portal usually = full double-leg gantry, sometimes with cantilevers.

## 4. Jib Cranes

**Applications:** localized, repetitive workstation lifting — machine loading/unloading, welding cells, assembly, as a supplement under a bridge crane. **Industries:** machine shops, automotive, fabrication, warehousing. *(Source: iqsdirectory.com, mazzellacompanies.com, munckcranes.com, High)*

**Specs:** wall-mounted, wall-travelling, freestanding/floor-mounted (foundation), or mast-type; boom/span 8–30 ft (≈2–9 m); capacity to ~5 T typical (custom higher); rotation 180° (wall) to 360° (freestanding). *(Source: iqsdirectory.com "up to 5 tons, 8–30 ft," munckcranes.com "180°–360°," High)*

**Components:** mast/column, boom, rotation bearing, hoist + trolley, slewing drive (motorized) or manual push. **Accessories:** rotation stops, festoon, articulating booms (obstacle clearance). **Maintenance:** bearing lubrication, bolt-torque on foundation, hoist checks. **Safety:** foundation adequacy, rotation limits, load rating on boom.

**Buying considerations:** floor space, foundation vs wall structure, coverage arc. **Pain points:** inadequate wall/column strength for wall-mounted; foundation cost for freestanding.

**Buyer FAQ:** *Freestanding or wall?* Freestanding = 360° but needs foundation; wall = space-saving but limited arc and needs strong structure.

## 5. Floor / Workstation Cranes

**Applications:** light ergonomic handling on assembly lines, repetitive picks, enclosed-track systems. **Industries:** automotive assembly, electronics, light manufacturing. **Specs:** capacity ~0.125–2 T; enclosed-track (light rail) bridge & monorail; manual or powered; modular, low deadweight for easy movement. **Duty:** light. **Components:** enclosed steel/aluminium track, trolleys, light hoist, ergonomic controls. **Maintenance/Safety:** minimal — trolley wheels, hoist, end stops. **Buying considerations:** ergonomics, positioning precision, low headroom. **Pain points:** not for heavy or high-duty use. **Buyer FAQ:** *Difference from EOT?* Lighter, ergonomic, enclosed-track, workstation-scale. *(Confidence: Medium — general industry knowledge + product corpus)*

## 6. Monorail Cranes

**Applications:** point-to-point load transfer along a fixed path — assembly lines, process transfer, loading bays. **Industries:** automotive, process, warehousing. **Specs:** hoist travels on a single fixed beam (I-beam or enclosed track); capacity 0.5–10 T; can include switches, curves, turntables for routing. **Duty:** M3–M6. **Components:** runway beam, hoist/trolley, track switches, festoon/DSL. **Maintenance:** beam joint & switch inspection, trolley wheels, hoist. **Safety:** end stops, limits, earthing. **Buying considerations:** fixed path only; routing complexity (curves/switches). **Pain points:** inflexible once installed. **Buyer FAQ:** *Monorail vs EOT?* Monorail = one fixed line, no cross-travel; EOT covers a full rectangular area. *(Confidence: Medium)*

## 7. Hot Metal / Ladle Cranes

**Applications:** transporting ladles of molten metal, casting/teeming, charging — the highest-risk crane class. **Industries:** steel plants, foundries, metal casting. *(Source: voittocrane.com, hnksglobal.com, afecrane.com, High)*

**Specs:** capacity 5–500+ T; **duty M7–M8 / CMAA Class E–F / FEM A7–A8**; reinforced heat-resistant structure, dual/redundant hoisting & braking, heat shielding. *(Source: hnksglobal.com "FEM A7-A8, ISO M7-M8, CMAA E-F," streetcrane.com "up to M8/Class F," High)*

**Safety (critical):** **wire-rope factor min 8:1** and load ≤12.5% of rope breaking strength for molten metal (IS 3177 note); redundant brakes; overload protection; ladle must not be transported tilted; no personnel near track during ladle movement; 8:1 rope factor exceeds standard lifting. *(Source: revacranes.com IS 3177 note, afecrane.com "8:1 wire rope safety factor," scribd hot-metal safety standards, High)*

**Components:** twin reinforced girders, redundant main hoist reeving, heat-shielded ropes/electricals, fail-safe brakes, cabin with heat protection. **Maintenance:** frequent rope inspection (heat degradation), brake redundancy tests, structural fatigue monitoring. **Buying considerations:** governed by IS 4137 (heavy-duty steelwork) in addition to IS 807/3177; safety redundancy non-negotiable. **Pain points:** highest cost & liability; requires specialist engineering — a genuine differentiator/moat for makers who can certify it.

**Buyer FAQ:** *Why more expensive?* Redundant safety systems, heat resistance, M8 continuous duty, 8:1 rope factor. *Which standard?* IS 4137 + IS 807 + CMAA Class F.

## 8. Crane Components & Spare Parts

**Applications:** aftermarket for SVMH's own and competitors' cranes — recurring revenue, deeper client lock-in. **Items:** crab/trolley units, forged hooks (certified), DSL/shrouded busbars, pendant cables & stations, wire ropes, gearboxes (hardened helical), crane sheaves, rope drums, wheels, brakes (thruster/EM), S4 crane-duty motors, LT/CT drives, festoon systems, limit switches, overload limiters, radio remotes.

**Standards/safety:** forged hooks require certification; ropes per IS 3177 factors; festoon cables EPR per IS 9968; motors S4 crane duty; gears hardened low-carbon alloy steel (IS 3177 cl. 22). *(Source: revacranes.com Buyer's Guide checklist, High)*

**Buying considerations:** part compatibility across OEMs, lead time, certification. **Pain points (opportunity):** buyers struggle to find part numbers / compatibility online — a searchable spares catalog is rare in the Indian SME segment and a strong differentiator.

**Buyer FAQ:** *Can you supply spares for another brand's crane?* Often yes (crab units, hooks, busbars). *Are hooks certified?* Should carry test certificates.
